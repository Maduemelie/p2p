## Challenges

### [Low] Challenge 1: Dust Filtering Threshold Discontinuity & Boundary Quantities
- **Assumption challenged**: Filtering logic `qty < Math.max(2, safeAvgVol * 0.05)` strictly excludes dust below threshold while preserving exact boundary quantities $T \pm \epsilon$.
- **Attack scenario**: Ads with exact quantities at threshold $T = 5.0$ (for $\text{avgVol}=100$), $T = 2.0$ (kink point at $\text{avgVol}=40$), sub-floor quantities ($1.99$ USDT), institutional quantities ($50$ USDT for $\text{avgVol}=1000$), and 2,000 randomized floating-point ad quantities.
- **Blast radius**: If boundary checks used `<=`, exact minimum threshold ads would be falsely discarded; if dust filter failed, micro-dust ads would skew VWAP and Top-1 competitor reference rates.
- **Empirical verification result**: **PASS**. Quantities $< T$ are rejected, exact boundary quantities $\ge T$ are preserved, and invalid volumes (`0`, `-10`, `NaN`, `null`, `undefined`) safely default to 100 USDT ($T = 5.0$ USDT).

### [Low] Challenge 2: Trade Limit Filtering Boundaries & Bypass Controls
- **Assumption challenged**: Fiat trade amount bounds ($\text{safeAvgVol} \times \text{price}$) strictly adhere to `minAmount` / `minSingleTransAmount` and `maxAmount` / `maxSingleTransAmount`, while allowing bypass when `filterLimits = false`.
- **Attack scenario**: Tested lower boundary $\text{tradeAmount} = \text{minLmt} \pm 0.01$, upper boundary $\text{tradeAmount} = \text{maxLmt} \pm 0.01$, single-sided limits (only `minLmt` or only `maxLmt`), zero limits, and 2,000 fuzzed parameter sets.
- **Blast radius**: Incorrect boundary conditions could exclude valid executable competitor ads or include ads whose limits the user cannot fulfill.
- **Empirical verification result**: **PASS**. Boundary conditions match exact inequality specifications ($tradeAmount \ge minLmt$ and $tradeAmount \le maxLmt$), and `filterLimits: false` strictly disables limit filtering without bypassing dust filtering.

### [Low] Challenge 3: Cross-Feature Arbitrage Cycle Simulation & FIFO Cost Basis Invariants
- **Assumption challenged**: Consecutive buy and sell execution cycles maintain accounting parity, non-negative inventory, spread protection gates, and consistent FIFO cost basis evolution without floating-point accumulation drift.
- **Attack scenario**: Simulated 100 consecutive market cycles with fluctuating market depths, calculating `calculateBuyPricing`, executing buy trades, recalculating FIFO holding costs, calculating `calculateSellPricing`, and executing sell trades.
- **Blast radius**: If cost basis calculations lagged or suggested prices violated safety gates, arbitrageurs could sell at a loss or accumulate unhedged inventory.
- **Empirical verification result**: **PASS**. Throughout 100 complete round-trips:
  - `suggestedBuy` was strictly $\le \text{maxBuyPrice}$.
  - `suggestedSell` was strictly $\ge \text{targetSellPrice}$.
  - `breakEven` strictly matched $\text{costBasis} + (\text{outflowFee} / \text{safeAvgVol})$.
  - FIFO inventory returned to exactly 0.00 USDT with positive realized PnL.

### [Low] Challenge 4: UI & DOM Consistency and Taker vs Maker Perspective Alignment
- **Assumption challenged**: All DOM elements, badges, orderbook tables, and copy triggers comply with R1-R4 specifications without inverted sides or confusing taker/maker labels.
- **Attack scenario**: Audited DOM tree generated by `renderPricingView()`, tested dynamic badge classes under safe vs compressed conditions, and verified orderbook row click prefill direction mapping.
- **Blast radius**: Inverted labels or badges could cause traders to place buy orders under the sell tab or misinterpret market depth.
- **Empirical verification result**: **PASS**. All 22 mandatory DOM element IDs exist. Subtitles explicitly map Buy Ad to Bybit P2P "Sell" tab and Sell Ad to Bybit P2P "Buy" tab. Dynamic badges correctly toggle between `.badge-success` (Safe), `.badge-danger` (Compressed / Below Target), and `.badge-neutral` (Offline).
